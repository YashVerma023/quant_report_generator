01 CAGR — Compound Annual Growth Rate Return metric
Definition

The annualised rate at which the strategy grows. Smooths out day-to-day volatility into a single per-year figure for easy comparison. In this report we use a linear annualisation of the mean daily return (not geometric compounding), which is appropriate for an additive equity curve.

Formula used in this report
CAGR = mean(r_t) × N

where
  r_t = daily return fraction
        = sum_mtm_t / sum_allocation_t
  N   = trading days per year
        (derived from the data:
         distinct dates ÷ calendar years spanned)
Rating scale
RangeRatingInterpretation
< 5%PoorBarely beating inflation — reconsider the strategy
5% – 10%Below avgMarginal; fixed deposits offer similar returns with no risk
10% – 20%GoodSolid for equity strategies; in line with Nifty 50 historical CAGR (~12–14%)
20% – 35%Very GoodStrong outperformance — verify it's consistent across years
35% – 50%ExcellentTop-tier; confirm with Sharpe and drawdown before deploying capital
> 50%SuspiciousLikely overfitted or extreme risk taken — stress-test thoroughly
Interpretation notes

CAGR alone is incomplete — a 40% CAGR with a −50% max drawdown is not deployable. Always pair with Volatility and Calmar. A high CAGR driven by one outlier year should be treated with the same skepticism as a > 50% figure.

02 Cumulative Return Return metric
Definition

Total percentage gain (or loss) from the first to the last date in the dataset. Unlike CAGR it is not annualised — it simply shows the total growth over the observed period.

Formula used in this report
Cumulative Return = Σ r_t   (simple additive sum)

Equity curve (for charting)
  = running cumulative sum of r_t

Note: this is additive (not multiplicative/geometric).
Display: multiplied by 100 to show as %.
Rating scale (over a 3-year backtest)
RangeRatingInterpretation
< 20%PoorBarely growing — not worth the operational risk
20% – 50%Below avgModest growth; check why CAGR is low
50% – 100%GoodDecent performance over three years
100% – 200%Very GoodStrong multi-year growth
200% – 400%ExcellentTop-tier — validate for consistency
> 400%SuspiciousValidate carefully for overfitting or survivorship bias
Interpretation notes

Always state the time period alongside the cumulative return. 100% in 10 years ≠ 100% in 1 year. Pair with CAGR to understand the per-year equivalent.

03 Sharpe Ratio Risk-adjusted return
Definition

How much excess return you earn per unit of total risk (standard deviation). It penalises both upside and downside volatility equally. Higher is better.

Formula used in this report
Sharpe = (mean(r_t) − r_f / N) / std(r_t, ddof=1) × √N

where
  r_f = annual risk-free rate (0.0% in this report)
  N   = trading days per year
  std(ddof=1) = sample standard deviation

Annualises the per-day Sharpe by × √N.
Rating scale
RangeRatingInterpretation
< 0Very PoorLosing money vs the risk-free rate — stop or rethink
0.0 – 0.5PoorNot compensating for the risk taken
0.5 – 1.0AverageMarginal risk-reward; survivable but not deployable at scale
1.0 – 1.5GoodSolid risk-adjusted performance
1.5 – 2.0Very GoodStrong strategy — investor-ready
2.0 – 3.0ExcellentExceptional; top-decile among systematic strategies
> 3.0SuspiciousLikely overfitting or a short/cherry-picked backtest
Interpretation notes

Sharpe assumes returns are normally distributed — not always true for options/intraday strategies. It punishes upside variance the same as downside variance, which can underrate strategies with positive skew. Always pair with Sortino for a fairer picture.

04 Sortino Ratio Downside risk-adjusted return
Definition

A refined version of the Sharpe Ratio that only penalises downside volatility (negative returns), not upside gains. More realistic for asymmetric strategies.

Formula used in this report
Sortino = (mean(r_t) − r_f / N) / DD × √N

Downside deviation (DD):
  DD = √( mean( min(r_t, 0)² ) )
     = root-mean-square of negative daily returns
       (zero target; flat/zero days treated as losses)

r_f = 0.0% in this report.
Rating scale
RangeRatingInterpretation
< 0Very PoorStrategy destroys capital on bad days faster than it earns on good days
0.0 – 0.5PoorHigh downside risk, low reward
0.5 – 1.0AverageModerate downside management
1.0 – 1.5GoodAcceptable downside risk profile
1.5 – 2.5Very GoodStrong downside protection
2.5 – 3.5ExcellentNear-ideal risk management
> 3.5SuspiciousVerify with more data and out-of-sample periods
Interpretation notes

Sortino is always ≥ Sharpe (its denominator is smaller). A Sortino of 2.0 alongside a Sharpe of 1.0 suggests strong upside skew — the strategy's volatility mostly comes from winning days, which is desirable.

05 Calmar Ratio Return vs worst loss
Definition

How much annual return you earn for the worst pain you endure. Answers the question: "Is the CAGR worth the max drawdown I would have experienced?"

Formula used in this report
Calmar = CAGR / |Max Drawdown|

Both CAGR and Max Drawdown are computed on the
largest contiguous segment when breaks exist.

Max Drawdown is a negative fraction; taking |·|
gives a positive denominator.
Rating scale
RangeRatingInterpretation
< 0.5Very PoorMax drawdown far exceeds the annual gain — unacceptable
0.5 – 1.0PoorDrawdown not justified by returns
1.0 – 2.0AverageAcceptable return-to-drawdown trade-off
2.0 – 3.0GoodStrong performance with manageable pain
3.0 – 5.0Very GoodExcellent risk-reward
> 5.0ExcellentWorld-class; verify for overfitting and short backtest length
Interpretation notes

Calmar is sensitive to backtest length — a short period may not have hit the strategy's worst scenario yet. Best used with at least 3–5 years of data. A Calmar that looks great in a bull market may collapse in a drawdown-heavy period.

06 Annual Volatility Risk / uncertainty
Definition

How much the strategy's daily returns fluctuate, expressed as an annualised standard deviation. Higher volatility = more uncertainty, more emotional stress, and higher margin requirements in live trading.

Formula used in this report
Annual Volatility = std(r_t, ddof=1) × √N

where
  std(ddof=1) = sample standard deviation of daily returns
  N           = trading days per year

Display: multiplied by 100 to show as %.
Rating scale (equity strategies)
RangeRatingInterpretation
< 5%Very LowBond-like stability; possibly too conservative for an options strategy
5% – 10%LowVery stable — good for conservative deployment
10% – 15%ModerateTypical for balanced large-cap equity (Nifty ≈ 14–18% in normal years)
15% – 25%HighAggressive strategy; ensure Sharpe justifies the turbulence
25% – 40%Very HighStressful to hold; verify returns more than compensate
> 40%ExtremeHigh risk of ruin; review position sizing immediately
Interpretation notes

Low volatility alone is not good — a flat-line strategy with 0% vol and 0% return is useless. Always pair with returns via the Sharpe Ratio. Intraday strategies typically have lower annualised vol than equivalent overnight-hold strategies.

07 Max Drawdown Worst-case loss
Definition

The largest peak-to-trough decline in the portfolio's equity curve. It represents the worst-case loss a trader would have suffered if they entered at the peak and exited at the lowest subsequent point.

Formula used in this report
Equity curve: E_t = Σ r_0..t   (additive cumsum)

Peak at t:    P_t = max(E_0 .. E_t)

Drawdown:     D_t = E_t − P_t   (always ≤ 0)

Max Drawdown = min(D_t)   (most negative value)

Computed on the largest contiguous segment.
Display: multiplied by 100 to show as %.
Rating scale (lower magnitude = better)
Range (absolute)RatingInterpretation
0% – 5%ExcellentVery rare; near-perfect capital protection
5% – 10%Very GoodOutstanding drawdown control
10% – 20%GoodAcceptable for most equity strategies
20% – 30%AverageRequires strong conviction to hold through
30% – 50%PoorVery difficult to sustain psychologically
> 50%Very PoorHalf your capital gone; a 100% gain needed just to break even
Interpretation notes

Max Drawdown does not tell you how long the drawdown lasted (see Avg Drawdown Days). Most retail traders quit strategies during drawdown — knowing the depth of the worst historical drawdown prepares you psychologically. A −50% drawdown requires a +100% gain just to recover.

08 Avg Drawdown Days Recovery time
Definition

The average number of trading days the strategy spends underwater (below a previous equity peak) across all drawdown episodes. It measures how quickly the strategy recovers from losses.

Formula used in this report
An "underwater episode" starts when E_t drops
below its prior peak (P_t) and ends the first day
E_t recovers to or above P_t.

Length of episode i = number of trading days
                      in that underwater period.

Avg Drawdown Days = mean(length_i)
                    over all episodes.

Returns 0 if no drawdown episodes exist.
Computed on the largest contiguous segment.
Rating scale
RangeRatingInterpretation
< 5 daysExcellentRecovers almost immediately; very low stress
5 – 15 daysVery GoodShort recoveries; manageable psychologically
15 – 30 daysGoodAbout 1-month average recovery
30 – 60 daysAverage1–2 months underwater; tests patience
60 – 120 daysPoorQuarterly drawdowns; hard to defend to investors
> 120 daysVery PoorLong underwater periods; strategy review needed
Interpretation notes

A short Avg Drawdown Days combined with a low Max Drawdown signals a robust strategy. Long average days even with a small Max Drawdown means the strategy slowly grinds lower and takes a long time to recover — painful for monthly/quarterly performance reviews.

09 Kelly Criterion Optimal position sizing
Definition

The mathematically optimal fraction of capital to risk on each trade to maximise long-run geometric growth while avoiding ruin. It balances aggression with safety. A positive value is actionable; a negative value means do not trade this setup.

Formula used in this report
Kelly % = W − (1 − W) / R

where
  W = win rate
    = count(r_t > 0) / total days
  R = win/loss ratio
    = mean(r_t | r_t > 0) / mean(|r_t| | r_t ≤ 0)

Note: flat/zero days (r_t = 0) count as losses,
which can deflate R — interpret conservatively.

Practical rule: use ½ Kelly or ¼ Kelly in live
trading to account for model estimation error.
Rating scale (recommended Kelly % per trade)
RangeRatingInterpretation
< 0%Do not tradeNegative Kelly = negative expected value at any position size
0% – 5%Very conservativeToo small; minimal compounding benefit
5% – 10%ConservativeSafe starting range for new/unvalidated strategies
10% – 20%Moderate ✓Balanced risk-reward; recommended zone for live deployment
20% – 30%AggressiveUse Half-Kelly at most; equity curve will be volatile
30% – 50%Very AggressiveUse Quarter-Kelly maximum; near-certain large drawdowns at full Kelly
> 50%ExtremeFull Kelly here leads to near-certain ruin — verify the inputs
Interpretation notes

Kelly assumes a constant win-rate and R-ratio across all periods — rarely true in live markets. Full Kelly produces extreme equity-curve volatility. Most professionals use Half-Kelly (Kelly / 2) or Quarter-Kelly to reduce variance and protect against model estimation errors. Always treat Kelly as an upper bound, not a target.

10 Rolling Sharpe Ratio Consistency check
Definition

The Sharpe Ratio computed over a rolling window (default: 63 trading days ≈ 1 quarter) moving across the entire history. It shows whether risk-adjusted performance is consistent over time or only good during certain market regimes.

Formula used in this report
At each day t (after the first window fills):

  Rolling Sharpe_t
    = mean(r_{t−W+1} .. r_t) / std(r_{t−W+1} .. r_t, ddof=1)
      × √N

where W = 63 (rolling window in trading days)
      N = trading days per year
      r_f = 0 in this report

The chart line begins only after the first W days,
which is why it starts later than the equity curve.
Rating scale (for the rolling values across time)
PatternRatingInterpretation
Always negativeVery PoorStrategy consistently destroys value — do not run
Mostly < 0.5PoorUnreliable; high variability across regimes
Mostly 0.5 – 1.0AverageSome good periods, some bad; regime-dependent
Mostly 1.0 – 1.5GoodRelatively stable across conditions
Mostly > 1.5Very GoodConsistent outperformance across market cycles
Stable > 2.0ExcellentNear-ideal consistency — rare in practice
Key signal — consistency check

High variance in Rolling Sharpe → strategy only works in certain regimes; dangerous to run live without regime filters.
Low variance in Rolling Sharpe → strategy is robust across conditions; safer to deploy at scale.
A high overall Sharpe but high variance in the rolling series is a red flag — the average is being carried by a few exceptional periods. Rolling Sharpe dipping negative for extended stretches signals strategy failure, not just a drawdown.

Combining Metrics — The Full Scorecard

Return quality

CAGR + Sharpe Ratio — High CAGR + High Sharpe = consistent, risk-adjusted gains. High CAGR + Low Sharpe = returns driven by big lucky days, not edge.

Downside safety

Max Drawdown + Calmar + Avg Drawdown Days — Low drawdown + High Calmar + Low days = capital is well-protected and recovers quickly. This trio tells you the "pain profile" of the strategy.

Consistency check

Rolling Sharpe + Annual Volatility — Stable Rolling Sharpe + Low Vol = strategy works across market regimes. Volatile Rolling Sharpe = regime-dependent; size down or add regime filters.

Skew quality

Sharpe vs Sortino gap — A large Sortino/Sharpe gap (Sortino significantly higher) indicates the strategy's volatility mostly comes from positive days — a desirable characteristic for options-selling strategies.

Position sizing

Kelly Criterion — Use as an upper bound, not a target. Apply ½ Kelly or ¼ Kelly in live trading. If Kelly is negative, do not increase position size regardless of other metrics.

Full scorecard

No strategy should be deployed live on 1–2 metrics alone. A strategy needs to pass on all 10: acceptable CAGR, Sharpe ≥ 1.0, Sortino ≥ 1.5, Max DD < 20%, Calmar ≥ 1.5, Avg DD Days < 30, positive Kelly, and a stable Rolling Sharpe chart.
