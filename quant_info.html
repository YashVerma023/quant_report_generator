Data Pipeline & Processed Columns

Step 1 — Broker Whitelist (applied before any aggregation)

Only rows where broker (case-insensitive) matches one of the following 5 brokers are retained. All other brokers are dropped across every algo.

Step 2 — Allocation Scaling

processed_allocation = raw_allocation × 100

Raw allocation values in the source file represent 1% of actual capital. Multiplying by 100 gives the true rupee allocation for each user-algo-date record.

01. CAGR — Compound Annual Growth Rate
01
CAGR — Compound Annual Growth Rate

The annualised rate at which an investment grows over the period. Smooths volatility into a single consistent annual figure. Note: this implementation uses linear annualisation (mean daily return × N), not geometric compounding.

daily_return (r_t) = sum_mtm_t ÷ sum_allocation_t N = distinct trading dates ÷ calendar years spanned CAGR = mean(r_t) × N Displayed as percentage. e.g. CAGR = 0.0012 × 252 = 30.24%
RangeRatingInterpretation
< 5%PoorBarely beating inflation
5% – 10%Below AvgWeak equity returns
10% – 20%GoodSolid for equity strategies
20% – 35%Very GoodStrong outperformance
35% – 50%ExcellentTop-tier — verify consistency
> 50%SuspiciousLikely overfitted or extreme risk
Notes: CAGR alone doesn't reveal volatility. Pair with Sharpe. Benchmark: Nifty 50 ~12–14% CAGR historically. High CAGR with high drawdown is dangerous — check Calmar together.
02. Cumulative Return
02
Cumulative Return

Total gain or loss from first to last date. Unlike CAGR, it does NOT annualise — it shows the total absolute gain. The equity curve is the running cumulative sum of daily returns.

Equity curve = cumsum(r_t) (additive, not compounded) Cumulative Return = Σ r_t = equity_curve[-1] Displayed as percentage. e.g. 0.45 → 45%
Range (over ~3-yr backtest)RatingInterpretation
< 20%PoorBarely growing
20% – 50%Below AvgModest growth
50% – 100%GoodDecent multi-year performance
100% – 200%Very GoodStrong multi-year growth
200% – 400%ExcellentTop-tier strategy
> 400%SuspiciousValidate carefully for overfitting
Notes: Always read alongside the time period — 100% in 10 years ≠ 100% in 1 year. Pair with CAGR for a per-year equivalent.
03. Sharpe Ratio
03
Sharpe Ratio

Risk-adjusted return: how much excess return you earn per unit of total volatility. Higher is better. Penalises both upside and downside volatility equally.

rf_daily = risk_free_annual ÷ N Sharpe = (mean(r_t) − rf_daily) ÷ std(r_t, ddof=1) × √N N = derived trading days per year
RangeRatingInterpretation
< 0Very PoorLosing money vs risk-free
0.0 – 0.5PoorNot compensating for risk
0.5 – 1.0AverageMarginal risk-reward
1.0 – 1.5GoodSolid risk-adjusted performance
1.5 – 2.0Very GoodStrong strategy
2.0 – 3.0ExcellentExceptional performance
> 3.0SuspiciousMay indicate overfitting
Notes: Assumes normally distributed returns. Use Sortino alongside for strategies with asymmetric returns. Default risk-free rate in this system = 0% (configurable via --risk-free-annual).
04. Sortino Ratio
04
Sortino Ratio

A refined Sharpe that penalises only downside (negative-day) volatility. More realistic for strategies with positive outliers.

Downside Deviation (DD) = √( mean( min(r_t, 0)² ) ) — zero/flat days are treated as losses in the denominator Sortino = (mean(r_t) − rf_daily) ÷ DD × √N
RangeRatingInterpretation
< 0Very PoorStrategy destroys capital on bad days
0.0 – 0.5PoorHigh downside risk, low reward
0.5 – 1.0AverageModerate downside management
1.0 – 1.5GoodAcceptable downside profile
1.5 – 2.5Very GoodStrong downside protection
2.5 – 3.5ExcellentNear-ideal risk management
> 3.5SuspiciousVerify with more data
Notes: Sortino is always ≥ Sharpe (smaller denominator). A Sortino of 2.0 with Sharpe 1.0 suggests good upside skew. Ideal for momentum/trend strategies.
05. Calmar Ratio
05
Calmar Ratio

Annual return relative to the worst loss. Answers: "How much annual return am I getting for the worst pain I endured?"

Calmar = CAGR ÷ |Max Drawdown| Computed on the largest contiguous segment (no data gaps > max_gap_days).
RangeRatingInterpretation
< 0.5Very PoorMax drawdown far too large vs returns
0.5 – 1.0PoorDrawdown not justified by returns
1.0 – 2.0AverageAcceptable return-to-drawdown trade-off
2.0 – 3.0GoodStrong with manageable pain
3.0 – 5.0Very GoodExcellent risk-reward
> 5.0ExcellentWorld-class — verify for overfitting
Notes: Very sensitive to backtest length. Shorter backtests may not have hit worst drawdown scenarios yet. Best used with ≥ 3 years of data.
06. Annual Volatility
06
Annual Volatility

How much the strategy's returns fluctuate, annualised from daily data. Higher volatility = more uncertainty and emotional stress.

Annual Volatility = std(r_t, ddof=1) × √N N = derived trading days per year (≈ 252 for typical markets)
RangeRatingInterpretation
< 5%Very LowBond-like, possibly too conservative
5% – 10%LowVery stable — good for conservative traders
10% – 15%ModerateTypical for balanced equity
15% – 25%HighAggressive strategy, needs strong nerves
25% – 40%Very HighStressful — ensure returns justify this
> 40%ExtremeHigh risk of ruin; review position sizing
Notes: Low volatility alone is NOT good — pair with returns (Sharpe). Nifty 50 volatility ≈ 14–18% in normal years. Intraday strategies typically show lower annualised vol.
07. Max Drawdown
07
Max Drawdown

Largest peak-to-trough decline in the additive equity curve. Represents the worst loss a trader would have suffered entering at the peak and exiting at the trough.

Equity curve = cumsum(r_t) (additive) Peak_t = max(equity_0 … equity_t) Drawdown_t = equity_t − Peak_t Max Drawdown = min(Drawdown_t) ← most negative value
Range (lower magnitude = better)RatingInterpretation
0% – 5%ExcellentNear-perfect capital protection
5% – 10%Very GoodOutstanding drawdown control
10% – 20%GoodAcceptable for most equity strategies
20% – 30%AverageRequires strong conviction to hold
30% – 50%PoorPsychologically very difficult
> 50%Very PoorHalf capital gone; unacceptable
Notes: 50% drawdown requires a 100% gain just to break even. Max Drawdown tells you the worst loss — not how long it lasted (see Avg Drawdown Days). Most retail traders quit strategies during drawdowns.
08. Avg Drawdown Days
08
Avg Drawdown Days

Average number of trading days spent in a drawdown (equity below a prior peak). Measures recovery time and persistence of losses.

For each drawdown episode: episode_start = first day equity falls below prior peak episode_end = first day equity recovers to (or above) prior peak Avg Drawdown Days = mean(episode lengths in trading days)
RangeRatingInterpretation
< 5 daysExcellentRecovers almost immediately
5 – 15 daysVery GoodShort recoveries — low stress
15 – 30 daysGood~1-month average recovery
30 – 60 daysAverage1–2 months underwater; needs patience
60 – 120 daysPoorQuarterly drawdowns; psychologically hard
> 120 daysVery PoorLong underwater periods; strategy review needed
Notes: Short avg drawdown days + low max drawdown = robust strategy. Long avg days even with low max drawdown suggests slow recovery. Critical for fund managers with periodic performance reviews.
09. Kelly Criterion
09
Kelly Criterion

Optimal fraction of capital to risk per trade to maximise long-term growth while avoiding ruin. Balances aggression with safety.

W = win rate = count(r_t > 0) ÷ count(r_t ≠ 0) R = win/loss ratio = mean(r_t | r_t > 0) ÷ |mean(r_t | r_t < 0)| Kelly % = W − (1 − W) ÷ R Negative Kelly → do NOT size up; this setup has negative expectancy.
Recommended Kelly % per tradeRatingInterpretation
< 5%Very ConservativeToo small; minimal compounding
5% – 10%ConservativeSafe starting range for new strategies
10% – 20%ModerateBalanced risk-reward — recommended zone
20% – 30%AggressiveHigh risk — use Half-Kelly at most
30% – 50%Very AggressiveUse ¼ Kelly only; dangerous at full
> 50%ExtremeFull Kelly here = near-certain ruin
Practical rule: Most professionals use Half-Kelly (Kelly ÷ 2) to reduce variance and protect against model estimation errors. Full Kelly leads to extreme equity curve volatility. Negative Kelly means avoid this trade setup entirely.
10. Rolling Sharpe
10
Rolling Sharpe

Sharpe Ratio calculated over a rolling window (default: 63 trading days ≈ 1 quarter) across the full period. Shows whether performance is consistent over time or only good during certain regimes.

At each day T (once window is filled): window_returns = r_{T−W+1} … r_T (W = rolling window, default 63 days) Rolling Sharpe_T = (mean(window_returns) − rf_daily) ÷ std(window_returns, ddof=1) × √N
Pattern across timeRatingInterpretation
Always negativeVery PoorStrategy consistently destroys value
Mostly < 0.5PoorUnreliable; high variability
Mostly 0.5 – 1.0AverageSome good periods, some bad
Mostly 1.0 – 1.5GoodRelatively stable performance
Mostly > 1.5Very GoodConsistent outperformance
Stable & > 2.0ExcellentNear-ideal consistency
Consistency check: High variance in Rolling Sharpe → strategy only works in certain market regimes. Low variance → robust across all conditions. Look for strategies where Rolling Sharpe rarely goes below 0.5. Extended dips to negative = strategy failure signal. Best viewed as a time-series chart, not a single number.
